# 6. Why your health insurance premium keeps going up: medical inflation in India

**Medical costs in India are rising at roughly two to three times the rate of general inflation, and premiums follow.**

**The numbers.** Aon's *2026 Global Medical Trend Rates Report* projects that employee medical plan costs in India will rise 11.5% in 2026, after a projected 13% in 2025, against a global average of 9.8% \[1\]. "Medical trend" is the yearly rise in medical plan cost per employee \[1\]. Milliman's actuaries put India's medical trend at 12% in 2024, against general inflation of 4.2% in 2025 \[2\].

**What drives it.** Cardiovascular disease and hypertension have the greatest impact on claims in India; cancer and gastrointestinal conditions are the other top cost drivers \[1\]. Newer, costlier treatments and more people using their cover add to it.

**What it means for you.** If your employer's group cover shrinks or you leave the job, you buy your own policy at prices that rise every year, and the rises bite hardest in old age. In January 2025 the regulator, IRDAI, told insurers not to raise premiums on individual health policies for people above 60 by more than 10% a year without consulting it first \[3\]. That cap protects seniors, but 10% a year still doubles a premium in about seven years.

> Owner check: the Aon and Milliman figures measure employer plan costs, not retail premiums. Don't describe them as "premiums rose 11.5%". The IRDAI cap is verified against the circular itself \[4\].
