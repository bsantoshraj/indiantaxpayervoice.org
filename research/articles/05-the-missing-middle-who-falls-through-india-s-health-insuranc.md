# 5. The "missing middle": who falls through India's health insurance gap

**The poorest have Ayushman Bharat. The well-off buy private cover. In between, about 40 crore people had no financial protection for health at all, NITI Aayog found \[1\].**

**The three layers.** NITI Aayog's 2021 report *Health Insurance for India's Missing Middle* split the population into three groups \[1\]:

- **Bottom 50% (around 70 crore people):** hospital cover through PM-JAY and state government schemes.
- **About 20% (25 crore):** social health insurance (such as ESIC and government employee schemes) and private voluntary insurance.
- **At least 30% (40 crore):** no financial protection for health. The report says the real gap is larger, because of coverage gaps in PM-JAY and overlap between schemes.

**Who is in the middle.** Mostly the self-employed and informal workers in rural areas, and a broad mix of informal, semi-formal and formal workers in cities \[1\]. Private insurance "is designed for high-income groups" and costs at least two to three times what this group can afford, while ESIC and PM-JAY are closed to the general public \[1\].

**What has changed since.** PM-JAY gives eligible families up to ₹5 lakh a year for hospital care and covers about 12 crore families, nearly 55 crore people \[2\]. In September 2024 the Cabinet extended it to everyone aged 70 and above, regardless of income, adding about 4.5 crore families and 6 crore senior citizens \[2\]\[3\]. That helps older people; for working-age taxpayers in the middle, the gap NITI Aayog described is still largely theirs to fill.
