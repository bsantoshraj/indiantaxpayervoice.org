# 3. Where every rupee of the Union Budget comes from, and where it goes

**Income tax brings in 21 paise of every rupee the Centre receives, more than any other tax. Interest on past borrowing takes 20 paise of every rupee it spends.**

These are the Union Budget 2026-27 figures, from the Budget documents as reported by PTI ([Siasat, PTI](https://www.siasat.com/taxes-to-bring-in-64-paise-of-every-rupee-govt-earns-union-budget-2026-3334946/)).

**Where it comes from (paise per rupee)**

| Source | Paise |
| --- | --- |
| Borrowings and other liabilities | 24 |
| Income tax | 21 |
| Corporation tax | 18 |
| GST | 15 |
| Non-tax revenue | 10 |
| Excise duty | 6 |
| Customs | 4 |
| Non-debt capital receipts | 2 |

**Where it goes (paise per rupee)**

| Item | Paise |
| --- | --- |
| States' share of taxes and duties | 22 |
| Interest payments | 20 |
| Central sector schemes | 17 |
| Defence | 11 |
| Centrally sponsored schemes | 8 |
| Finance Commission and other transfers | 7 |
| Other expenditure | 7 |
| Subsidies | 6 |
| Pensions | 2 |

**What stands out.** Taxes fund 64 paise of each rupee; almost a quarter is borrowed. Interest on earlier borrowing (20 paise) costs nearly twice defence (11 paise). Health and education aren't separate lines here: most of that spending sits inside the scheme lines and with the states, which get 22 paise plus transfers.
