# 11. "No tax up to ₹12 lakh": who it helps, who pays, and the cliff at ₹12.75 lakh

**The ₹12 lakh rebate covers most salaried people who file. But income tax is paid mainly by a small group far above that line: in 2022-23, 1.4% of individual returns paid half of all individual income tax. And anyone who crosses ₹12 lakh finds tax suddenly counted from ₹4 lakh.**

### Who earns what: salaried filers, AY 2023-24

The Central Board of Direct Taxes' latest return statistics cover 7,54,61,286 returns filed by individuals for AY 2023-24, on income earned in 2022-23 \[1\]. Of these, 3,79,64,804 reported salary income. All figures below are calculated from that report's tables \[1\]\[2\].

### Who got how much relief in Budget 2025

Budget 2025 did two things under the new regime: it lowered the slab rates for everyone, and it raised the zero-tax rebate line from ₹7 lakh to ₹12 lakh \[3\]. Tax on the same taxable income, before and after (our calculation from the old and new slabs \[3\]\[4\]; excludes 4% cess):

| Taxable income | Tax before | Tax after | Saving | Cut |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ₹12 lakh | ₹80,000 | ₹0 | ₹80,000 | 100% |
| ₹16 lakh | ₹1,70,000 | ₹1,20,000 | ₹50,000 | 29% |
| ₹20 lakh | ₹2,90,000 | ₹2,00,000 | ₹90,000 | 31% |
| ₹25 lakh | ₹4,40,000 | ₹3,30,000 | ₹1,10,000 | 25% |
| ₹50 lakh | ₹11,90,000 | ₹10,80,000 | ₹1,10,000 | 9% |

The people who pay most of the tax did get relief, through the slabs: up to ₹1.1 lakh a year. But as a share of their tax it shrinks fast, from 100% at ₹12 lakh to 9% at ₹50 lakh. A person at ₹16 lakh saves less (₹50,000) than one at ₹12 lakh (₹80,000). The rebate itself, the part the headline is about, gives nothing to anyone above the line.

### The cliff: cross ₹12 lakh and the meter starts at ₹4 lakh

The ₹12 lakh is not a tax-free allowance. It is a *rebate* that cancels the tax only while taxable income stays at or below ₹12 lakh \[4\]. Cross it, and the rebate is gone: tax is worked out on everything above ₹4 lakh, at 5% from ₹4–8 lakh, 10% from ₹8–12 lakh and 15% beyond \[4\]. That is ₹60,000 owed on the first ₹12 lakh alone.

To stop a one-rupee raise costing ₹60,000, *marginal relief* caps the tax at the amount by which income exceeds ₹12 lakh \[5\]\[6\]. But the 4% cess is added on top \[4\]\[5\], so in that band every extra rupee costs about ₹1.04 in tax. Relief runs out at about ₹12.71 lakh of taxable income, where the normal slab tax and the cap meet (our calculation).

**For a salaried person on the new regime (our calculation from the official slabs \[4\])**

| Gross salary | Taxable income | Tax + 4% cess | Take-home after income tax |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ₹12,75,000 | ₹12,00,000 | ₹0 | ₹12,75,000 |
| ₹13,00,000 | ₹12,25,000 | ₹26,000 | ₹12,74,000 |
| ₹13,45,600 | ₹12,70,600 | ₹73,414 | ₹12,72,186 |
| ₹15,00,000 | ₹14,25,000 | ₹97,500 | ₹14,02,500 |
| ₹20,75,000 | ₹20,00,000 | ₹2,08,000 | ₹18,67,000 |

A raise from ₹12.75 lakh to anywhere up to about ₹13.49 lakh leaves you with *less* take-home pay than before. You need roughly ₹14 lakh to be clearly better off.

### The other fine print

1. **New regime only.** Under the old regime the rebate is ₹12,500, up to ₹5 lakh \[4\].
2. **Residents only.** Non-residents get no rebate \[4\]\[5\].
3. **Capital gains not covered.** The rebate cannot be set against tax on equity gains (sections 111A and 112A) or lottery winnings \[5\].
4. **Few deductions.** The new regime drops 80C, 80D (health insurance) and HRA; the employer's NPS contribution (up to 14% of salary) is one of the few left \[4\]. It is also the main legal way to pull taxable income back under ₹12 lakh.
5. **Zero income tax is not zero tax.** Everyone still pays GST (Article 10).

**The honest summary.** For most salaried people who file, ₹12 lakh really does mean no income tax. The people who pay most of the tax, roughly the top 10% of filers, got relief only through lower slabs, and it shrinks as their tax grows. And anyone who crosses the line finds tax suddenly counted from ₹4 lakh, with a band where a raise lowers take-home pay.
