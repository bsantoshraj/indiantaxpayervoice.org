# 4. Indians still pay 43 paise of every health rupee from their own pocket

**The latest National Health Accounts show out-of-pocket spending rising again, after a decade of decline.**

**The long fall.** Out-of-pocket expenditure (OOPE), meaning what households pay directly at the hospital, pharmacy or lab, fell from 62.6% of total health expenditure in 2014-15 to 39.4% in 2021-22. Over the same period the government's share rose from 29% to 48% ([PIB, NHA 2020-21 and 2021-22](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2024/oct/doc2024104408201.pdf), Oct 2024).

**The reversal.** NHA 2022-23, released by the Union Health Ministry on 27 May 2026, shows OOPE back up to 43.4% of total health expenditure ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/health/as-govt-cut-health-spends-post-covid-indians-paid-more-out-of-pocket-in-2022-23-reversing-10-yr-trend/2943692/)). Per person, OOPE rose from ₹2,600 to ₹2,767. Government health expenditure fell from ₹4,34,163 crore to ₹3,85,332 crore, and its share of the total dropped to 43.7% ([South First](https://thesouthfirst.com/health/as-public-health-spending-fell-in-2022-23-indians-paid-6-4-more-from-their-own-pockets/)).

**In context.** Total health spending in 2022-23 was ₹8,81,359 crore, about 3.4% of GDP or ₹6,373 per person (South First). Government spending was 1.48% of GDP (ThePrint). Part of the 2021-22 peak was Covid spending, so the drop is partly a return to normal. Either way, a family still meets more than two-fifths of the country's health bill directly.

> How to read this: every 2022-23 figure is checked against the official *National Health Accounts Estimates for India 2022-23* \[4\]. Government spending of 1.48% of GDP uses the new GDP series; on the old series it is 1.43%.
