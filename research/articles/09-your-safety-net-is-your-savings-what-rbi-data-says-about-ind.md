# 9. Your safety net is your savings: what RBI data says about Indian households

**With no unemployment benefit for most earners (Article 1) and high out-of-pocket health costs (Article 4), Indian households carry their own risk. RBI data shows how much financial cushion they are building, and how much of it is offset by borrowing.**

**Household financial flows, % of GDP** \[1\]

| Year | Financial assets added | Financial liabilities (borrowing) | Net financial savings |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2022-23 | 11.5 | 6.1 | 5.3 |
| 2023-24 | 12.4 | 6.6 | 5.8 |
| 2024-25 | 11.9 | 4.9 | 7.0 |

**What it shows.** In 2022-23 and 2023-24, households borrowed roughly half as much as they saved in financial assets: more than six rupees of new borrowing for every twelve put aside. Net financial savings rose to 7.0% of GDP in 2024-25, but mainly because borrowing slowed (from 6.6% to 4.9% of GDP), not because households saved more; financial assets added actually fell from 12.4% to 11.9% \[1\].

**Why it matters for taxpayers.** Savings are a family's own insurance: against job loss, illness and old age. When borrowing eats into them, a single shock can mean selling assets or taking more debt. Our survey asks how prepared people feel, and what role they think public safety nets should play.

> Owner check: these use RBI's new GDP series (base year 2022-23) and will be revised when the 2024-25 National Accounts are final \[1\]. Older articles quoting "5.1%" or a "multi-decade low" use the old series or a different base (GNDI), so don't mix them.
