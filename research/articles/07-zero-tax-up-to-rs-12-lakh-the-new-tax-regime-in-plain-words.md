# 7. Zero tax up to ₹12 lakh: the new tax regime in plain words

**Under the new regime, a resident individual with taxable income up to ₹12 lakh pays no income tax. For a salaried person that is about ₹12.75 lakh of salary.**

**The slabs (new regime, AY 2026-27)** \[1\]

| Taxable income | Rate |
| --- | --- |
| Up to ₹4,00,000 | Nil |
| ₹4,00,001 – ₹8,00,000 | 5% |
| ₹8,00,001 – ₹12,00,000 | 10% |
| ₹12,00,001 – ₹16,00,000 | 15% |
| ₹16,00,001 – ₹20,00,000 | 20% |
| ₹20,00,001 – ₹24,00,000 | 25% |
| Above ₹24,00,000 | 30% |

**Why ₹12 lakh is tax-free.** Tax on exactly ₹12 lakh by these slabs is ₹60,000 (₹20,000 + ₹40,000). A rebate of up to ₹60,000 cancels it, so the tax due is nil for total income up to ₹12,00,000 \[2\]. The rebate does not apply to income taxed at special rates, such as some capital gains \[2\].

**Why ₹12.75 lakh for the salaried.** Salaried people also get a ₹75,000 standard deduction, so a salary of up to ₹12.75 lakh works out tax-free \[2\].

**What changed in 2026.** The Income-tax Act, 2025 replaced the 1961 Act from 1 April 2026. The rebate moved from section 87A to section 156, and Budget 2026 kept the slabs unchanged under both regimes \[2\]. Cess (4%) and any surcharge apply to tax due above these thresholds.

**The bigger picture.** Combined with Article 2, this means even fewer salaried earners will owe income tax. Those who still do earn above ₹12.75 lakh, and pay the 15%–30% slabs. What do they expect in return?
