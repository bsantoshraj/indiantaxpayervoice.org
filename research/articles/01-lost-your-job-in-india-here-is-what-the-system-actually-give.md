# 1. Lost your job in India? Here is what the system actually gives you

**Most salaried income-tax payers have no public unemployment benefit at all.** The one scheme that exists is narrow, short and limited to lower-paid workers.

**The only unemployment benefit: ABVKY.** The Employees' State Insurance Corporation (ESIC) runs the Atal Beemit Vyakti Kalyan Yojana. It pays 50% of wages for up to 90 days, once in a lifetime ([ESIC](https://esic.gov.in/abvky)). To qualify, you must have been in insured employment for at least a year before losing the job, with at least 78 days of contributions ([ESIC](https://esic.gov.in/abvky)). The scheme runs year to year; it was last extended to 30 June 2027 ([Angel One, July 2026](https://www.angelone.in/news/government-schemes/esic-extends-atal-beemit-vyakti-kalyan-yojana-till-june-2027-who-can-claim-unemployment-benefits)).

**Who it covers.** ESIC covers employees earning up to ₹21,000 a month, a limit unchanged since 1 January 2017 ([ESIC](https://www.esic.gov.in/coverage)). That is ₹2.52 lakh a year. Anyone earning more, which includes most people who pay income tax, is outside ESIC and has no unemployment benefit.

**What else there is.** Gratuity is paid when employment ends, but it rewards years already served, not the months needed to find new work. Since the labour codes took effect on 21 November 2025, fixed-term employees qualify for gratuity after one year ([DLA Piper](https://knowledge.dlapiper.com/dlapiperknowledge/globalemploymentlatestdevelopments/2025/government-of-india-notifies-the-labour-codes-ushers-a-new-era-of-compliances)). Beyond that, a laid-off employee depends on notice pay, any severance the employer offers, and their own savings.
