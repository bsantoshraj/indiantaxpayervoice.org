# 8. How India's public spending on health and education compares with other countries

**India's government spends about 1.3% of GDP on health, well short of its own 2.5% target and a fraction of what Brazil, China or Thailand spend. On education it is closer to its peers, but still short of its 6% goal.**

**Health: government spending as % of GDP (2023)** \[1\]

| Country | % of GDP |
| --- | --- |
| United States | 9.01 |
| United Kingdom | 8.99 |
| South Africa | 5.49 |
| Brazil | 4.29 |
| Thailand | 3.53 |
| China | 3.39 |
| Indonesia | 1.45 |
| **India** | **1.30** |
| Sri Lanka | 1.24 |
| Bangladesh | 0.31 |

The National Health Policy 2017 set a target of 2.5% of GDP in government health spending by 2025 \[3\]. India's own National Health Accounts put it at 1.84% in 2021-22 and 1.48% in 2022-23 (see Article 4) \[4\]. The World Bank figure differs slightly because it uses a common international method.

**Education: government spending as % of GDP (latest year)** \[2\]

| Country | % of GDP | Year |
| --- | --- | --- |
| South Africa | 6.02 | 2024 |
| United Kingdom | 5.91 | 2021 |
| Brazil | 5.62 | 2022 |
| United States | 5.42 | 2021 |
| **India** | **4.10** | 2022 |
| China | 3.90 | 2023 |
| Thailand | 2.52 | 2023 |
| Bangladesh | 2.03 | 2024 |
| Sri Lanka | 1.83 | 2023 |

The National Education Policy 2020 calls for public investment in education of at least 6% of GDP "at the earliest" \[5\]. Measured as Union and state budget allocations, Accountability Initiative put 2021-22 spending at 3.1% of GDP \[5\]; the World Bank's broader measure gives 4.1% for 2022 \[2\].

**Why it matters.** When public spending is low, families pay instead: private school fees, coaching and hospital bills. Taxpayers often pay twice, once through tax and again from their pocket.
